AEGIS: a drone-swarm protective dome

Oct 27, 2026 · 5 min read

TL;DR
  • A simulation of 24 small drones holding slots on a 40 m hemispherical shell around an asset. When a radar track is classified hostile, the best-placed drone leaves the shell to capture it and the rest re-space to close the gap.
  • Each 50 ms tick runs sense → track → assess → assign → guide → heal: IMM filters on noisy radar, Hungarian assignment by time to intercept, lead pursuit, and a fresh Fibonacci lattice whenever the shell changes.
  • Over 16 seeds of the four standard scenarios: 448/448 hostiles captured, 0 leaks, 0 benign objects touched. Against 36 hostiles and 24 drones, every drone makes exactly one capture before the shell runs out.

The problem: keep inbound objects from reaching a protected asset using a fleet of small, single-use interceptors and one radar at the asset. The radar is noisy and misses scans. Some inbound traffic is benign and must be left alone. Some threats weave, some come in faster than the drones can fly, and sometimes there are more threats than drones. The shell has to keep covering every direction while it spends drones.

The pipeline

  • Sense. Radar reports with range-dependent noise (0.3 m floor plus 0.004 m per metre of range) out to 320 m, and 3% missed scans. Friendly drones are filtered upstream (IFF).
  • Track. An IMM filter per track blends a cruise model and a maneuver model by likelihood, so straight flyers get tight estimates and weaving targets stay inside the association gate. Association is global nearest neighbour (Hungarian) with a chi-square gate. Tracks confirm after 3 hits and coast through short dropouts.
  • Assess. Project both the instantaneous and the smoothed mean velocity and take the more dangerous prediction. A track is hostile if its closest approach falls inside the shell. Classification waits until the velocity estimate has settled; once hostile, hysteresis keeps it hostile.
  • Assign. Hungarian assignment of free shell drones to hostile tracks, minimizing time to intercept. An intercept only counts if it lands before the threat reaches the core and within the 180 m engagement range. Threats less than 4 s from breaching get a two-drone salvo. A reserve of 4 stays on the shell unless a threat would otherwise go unanswered.
  • Guide. Lead pursuit to the predicted intercept point under a 30 m/s speed and 45 m/s² acceleration limit. Long-horizon aim uses the smoothed velocity, terminal aim the instantaneous one.
  • Heal. Whenever the set of on-station drones changes, lay out a fresh Fibonacci lattice (equal area per drone) and move drones to slots by minimum total travel, again with the Hungarian method.

One detail that mattered: capture is checked with swept-segment closest approach, not end-of-tick positions. At 60 m/s closing speed a single 50 ms tick covers 3 m, twice the 1.5 m capture radius, so checking only where things end up would let drones fly through their targets.

Results

Five scenarios ship with the simulator. A sweep over 16 seeds of each of the first four, 24 drones each:

saturation throws 36 hostiles (mixed speeds, 30% weaving) at 24 drones. Every drone makes exactly one capture before the shell runs out. That is the designed failure mode. The dome degrades by running out of drones, not by wasting them.

Known limits

  • Terminal accuracy against hard-maneuvering targets is the weakest link. A weaver can take several attempts. Augmented proportional navigation with a target-acceleration estimate would cut drone spend.
  • No drone-to-drone collision avoidance in flight. Shell slots are spaced apart, but interceptors fly straight through each other.
  • Drones are single-use. A recover-and-rearm cycle would turn the magazine limit into a throughput limit.
  • One sensor, at the asset. Distributed sensing and track fusion would add low-altitude coverage.

Run it

uv sync
uv run aegis raid                # narrate one scenario, write replays/raid.html
uv run aegis all --quiet         # every scenario, summaries only
uv run aegis surround --seed 3 --drones 32
uv run pytest

Open replays/<scenario>.html for a 3D replay (drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, scrub the timeline); append #t=8.5 to open at a given moment. The dependencies are NumPy and SciPy.
